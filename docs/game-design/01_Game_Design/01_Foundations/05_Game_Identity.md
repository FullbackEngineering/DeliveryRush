# Delivery Rush — Game Identity

## Identity Pillars

### Emotional Tone

**Playful urgency.** Fast-paced, exhilarating, low-pressure. The countdown timer creates urgency, but the tone is arcade-fun, not stressful. Each order completion is a small win; each crash is a quick learning moment. Missing an order after chaining 5 feels like "oof, one more try"—not rage-quit frustration. The game celebrates player skill without punishing harshly.

### Visual Identity

Bright, low-poly 3D (Crossy Road–inspired), portrait-oriented 3D low-angle chase camera (perspective, slightly overhead, looking ahead). Procedurally generated city: chunky, readable buildings, roads, and intersections at a glance. Warm color palette (soft greens, blues, ochres). Soft shadows, no sharp edges; all geometry is simple (boxes, extrusions, minimal triangles). UI is minimal—big tap targets, dark rounded panels, emoji glyphs for orders. No diegetic UI clutter; information floats cleanly on screen. Vehicle is a simple low-poly car shape; no excessive detail. Camera follows from slightly above-behind, letting player see upcoming traffic and intersections clearly. No motion blur or chromatic aberration; clarity over fancy effects.

### Audio Identity

Upbeat, minimalist electronic score using simple sine/square wave synth tones. Sound effects are *musical*—not realistic engine roars, but satisfying "beep" on steering confirm, "chime" on order delivery, "splash" on crash (no harsh noise). Dialog is absent (no voice). Silence is used purposefully: quiet moments before a tight corner, then satisfying SFX on a clean turn. Audio pacing matches action: calm during delivery wait, energetic during traffic weaving. Inspired by hyper-casual games (Alto's Adventures) and puzzle games (Tetris Effect)—tonal, not literal.

### Narrative Identity

Zero explicit narrative. No cutscenes, no exposition, no NPC dialog. Story is told implicitly via emoji order glyphs (pizza delivery = 🍕, grocery = 🛒, etc.). The player infers: "I'm a courier delivering meals and goods." Time pressure is the only narrative tension: "Can I finish this delivery before the timer expires?" Themes are emergent: persistence (retrying after a crash), focus (chasing a higher combo), and risk-taking (pushing speed to chain multipliers). No character arcs, no plot twists. Tone is matter-of-fact: deliver, earn, retry.

### Interaction Identity

Responsive and tight. Tap a button and it feels immediate; hold gas and acceleration ramps smoothly but snappily. Steering confirms with audio/haptic feedback. Input is always honored (buffered steering doesn't drop queued turns). Difficulty is fair—no invisible mechanics, no cheap surprises. Feedback is celebratory: screen shake on a tight corner success, coin pop-up on order completion, multiplier tick-up animation. Mistakes are forgiving enough to retry quickly (no long load times, no penalty screens). Challenge escalates gradually over a run (traffic density and order timers ramp over ~12 deliveries) but never feels unfair. Player always knows why they crashed (hit a car, missed a beacon, ran out of time).

## Anti-Identity (What This Game Is NOT)

- **Not grim or harsh.** Tone is playful, not punishing. Crashes are learning moments, not rage-inducing. UI is friendly and welcoming, not dark/serious. (The game is not Dark Souls.)
- **Not realistic or grounded.** Low-poly bright shapes, not photorealistic. AI traffic behaves predictably, not chaotically. City is readable, not dense/overwhelming. (The game is not a true-to-life driving sim.)
- **Not narrative-heavy or story-driven.** No cutscenes, no character arcs, no plot twists. Story exists only to justify deliveries. (The game is not a visual novel or RPG.)
- **Not complex or deep mechanically.** Three buttons, one core loop, one primary resource (combo). No skill trees, no loadouts, no system interactions. (The game is not a strategy game.)
- **Not competitive or multiplayer.** Solo-only (currently). No PvP, no leaderboards (planned post-launch). Focus is on personal high scores and self-improvement, not beating others. (The game is not a competitive multiplayer game.)

## Identity Coherence Check

Each major system reinforces the five pillars:

- **Throttle mechanic** → Interaction identity (responsive, tight, reward-driven timing decisions)
- **Combo multiplier** → Emotional tone (small wins add up; one miss stings; retry loop builds urgency)
- **Procedural city** → Visual identity (readable, procedurally distinct, no repetition; variety keeps runs fresh)
- **Order timer (shrinks over run)** → Emotional tone (escalating urgency, not sudden punish)
- **Touch-only UI, emoji glyphs** → Visual identity (minimal, playful, emoji-native, all-ages)
- **Upbeat synth audio, satisfying SFX** → Audio identity (musical, celebratory, zero harsh sounds)
- **Instant restart, no friction** → Interaction identity (player always feels in control, mistakes don't cascade into load screens)
- **Low-poly bright aesthetic** → Emotional tone (cheerful, not dark or oppressive)

Every decision (from turret timing to UI color to sound design) reinforces: *fast, playful, skill-rewarding, all-ages casual arcade.*
