# 02 — Player Personas (Delivery Rush)

Three personas cover the primary casual and secondary midcore/collector audiences.

---

## Persona 1: Alex (The Commute Gamer)

**Age**: 24  
**Occupation**: Junior Software Developer  
**Location**: Urban, USA  
**Gaming Platform**: iPhone 14 Pro  
**Gaming Tenure**: 10 years (lifelong gamer; transitioned from console to mobile)

### Play Context
- **When**: Weekday mornings (8–9am commute), lunch break (30 min), occasional evening wind-down (10 min before bed)
- **Where**: Public transit (bus, train), office lunch room, home couch
- **Session Duration**: 5–10 minutes typical; up to 20 min on slow weekends
- **Social Play**: Solo with weekly leaderboard checks; no multiplayer expectation

### Core Motivation
Quick arcade satisfaction during stolen moments. Alex wants *mastery without commitment*—a game that feels tight, responsive, and rewarding in a 5-minute window. No story, no grind, no pressure. Each run feels distinct due to procedurally generated cities and random order types.

### Goals
- Complete 3–5 successful delivery runs per session without crashing (personal skill mastery)
- Maintain a consistent top-50 spot on weekly leaderboard (social proof, low stakes)
- Unlock a new vehicle skin every 2–3 weeks (progression feel, visual novelty)

### Frustrations
- **Tutorial bloat**: If onboarding takes >1 minute, Alex closes the app. Expects instant-to-play.
- **Account friction**: Won't create an account or log in. Must work anonymously with local save.
- **Session timers**: Doesn't want artificial session limits. Can play 3 runs or 15 runs—game should support both.
- **Pay-to-win perception**: If faster cars cost money, feels cheap. Cosmetics OK; gameplay parity mandatory.

### Skills & Experience
- 10 years gaming; familiar with mobile arcade (Crossy Road, Alto's Adventure, Flappy Bird)
- Intermediate mechanical skill; can reflex-steer and combo-chain; not a speedrunner
- Learns by doing; dislikes text tutorials; prefers visual/audio feedback

### Play Behaviors
- **Speed-runs**: Plays multiple runs back-to-back, chasing personal best times
- **Cosmetic hunting**: Glances at unlocked skins; occasionally watches rewarded ad for coins to buy a new one
- **Leaderboard checking**: Checks weekly leaderboard after a good run; doesn't obsess over rank
- **Share moments**: Screenshots a 8× combo and shares on Discord; enjoys peer recognition

### Monetization Profile
- **Spending Tier**: Dolphin (~$5–10/quarter)
- **Purchase Drivers**: Occasional cosmetic (new vehicle skin), rewarded ads for coin multiplier (2×), no battle pass

### A Note About Alex
Alex is the core audience. If the core loop doesn't feel tight in the first 30 seconds, Alex uninstalls and never returns. Mitigation: Polish feel and juice to elite level; cosmetics are secondary to mechanical satisfaction. Every decision should pass: "Would Alex enjoy this in a 5-minute commute window?"

## Persona 2: Jordan (The Weekend Collector)

**Age**: 38  
**Occupation**: Project Manager, Parent of Two  
**Location**: Suburban USA  
**Gaming Platform**: iPad Air 5 (hand-me-down iPhone 11 for play)  
**Gaming Tenure**: 25 years (console player; returned to gaming 3 years ago via mobile)

### Play Context
- **When**: Saturday mornings (20–30 min while kids watch cartoons), occasional evening after kids sleep (15 min)
- **Where**: Living room couch, sometimes kitchen table during lunch prep
- **Session Duration**: 15–30 minutes (single sitting, not interruptible)
- **Social Play**: Doesn't play multiplayer; occasionally mentions the game to spouse; no Discord/community engagement

### Core Motivation
Low-pressure progression and cosmetic collection. Jordan wants to feel like they're *advancing* something—unlocking cars, completing card sets—without mandatory grinding or competitive pressure. Play is *relaxing*, not challenging. The city-generation novelty keeps runs fresh.

### Goals
- Unlock all vehicle cosmetics over 4–6 weeks (collection completeness)
- Reach an 8× combo at least once (manageable challenge; not speed-run tight)
- Play casually 2–3 times per week without feeling FOMO (sustainable habit)

### Frustrations
- **Grind walls**: If cosmetics require 50 runs to unlock, feels like homework, not play. Must be attainable in 5–10 runs.
- **Time pressure in-game**: Doesn't like feeling rushed during a run; prefers a generous run timer (60 sec is plenty).
- **Cosmetics locked behind pay**: Would pay for cosmetics but dislikes FOMO or "limited-time" mechanics. Wants to buy what they see, whenever they want.
- **Complex card synergies**: If card builds become too abstract, loses interest. Prefers clear "speed card = go faster" over deep optimization.

### Skills & Experience
- 25 years gaming; prefers casual puzzle/story games (Stardew Valley, Spiritfarer); avoids competitive multiplayer
- Mechanical skill: Low-to-moderate; can steer and avoid traffic; doesn't expect pixel-perfect combos
- Learns via explanation; reads card descriptions; prefers clear UI language over symbols

### Play Behaviors
- **Cosmetic checking**: First thing after launching: "Any new skins?" Unlocks vehicle skin eagerly; watches rewarded ad without hesitation
- **Card tinkering**: Equips different cards each run to experiment; likes the sense of "tuning" their car
- **Slow runs**: Plays conservatively, avoiding traffic; doesn't chase leaderboards; prefers steady 4–5× combos over risky 8× attempts
- **Story-free**: Skips any narrative; wants to jump into first delivery within 10 seconds

### Monetization Profile
- **Spending Tier**: Dolphin (~$8–15/month)
- **Purchase Drivers**: Vehicle cosmetics (new skins every few weeks), occasional cosmetic card, battle pass (if seasonal)

### A Note About Jordan
Jordan is the retention anchor. If Delivery Rush becomes too hard or too grindy, Jordan quits and doesn't return. Mitigation: Keep cosmetics attainable within 5–10 runs; don't raise difficulty beyond 8× combo skill floor; make cards fun without requiring deep optimization. Jordan plays *because it feels good*, not *despite* grinding.

## Persona 3: Casey (The Optimization Enthusiast)

**Age**: 32  
**Occupation**: Data Analyst  
**Location**: Urban Canada  
**Gaming Platform**: PC + iPhone 12 Pro Max  
**Gaming Tenure**: 20 years (former fighting-game competitor; now casual due to time constraints)

### Play Context
- **When**: Weekday evenings (1–2 hrs), weekend mornings (2–3 hrs if free)
- **Where**: Home office (PC + mobile), occasionally coffee shops (mobile only)
- **Session Duration**: 10–60 minute blocks (may chain 10–20 runs)
- **Social Play**: Interested in leaderboards and community comparisons; streams gameplay occasionally

### Core Motivation
Mastery of system depth and optimization. Casey wants to *understand* the combo mechanics, card synergies, and run economy at a deep level. Each run is a puzzle: "Given these random orders and traffic patterns, what's the optimal steering + card setup?" Leaderboards are significant.

### Goals
- Achieve a consistent 8× combo every run (near-peak mechanical performance)
- Optimize card builds for specific run types (e.g., high-value orders vs. chain-length runs)
- Rank in top-50 globally on weekly leaderboard (achievement signaling)
- Share builds and strategies with small community (Discord, Reddit)

### Frustrations
- **Opaque mechanics**: Won't accept "just steer and deliver." Wants to know exact combo multiplier formula, card stat effects, order-spawn patterns.
- **No endgame variance**: If every 8× combo run feels identical, loses interest fast. Needs new challenges (harder difficulties, card synergies to discover).
- **Leaderboard stability**: Resets or wipes would feel unfair; wants season-long persistence and clear metrics.
- **Pay-to-win cards**: Would pay for cosmetic-only passes; won't pay for gameplay-advantage cards (breaks optimization trust).

### Skills & Experience
- 20 years gaming; former competitive fighting-game player; high execution + system knowledge
- Mechanical skill: High; can reliably hit 7–8× combos with consistent steering
- Learns via deep-dive; reads patch notes, watches strategy videos, calculates damage models

### Play Behaviors
- **Speedrunning**: Times runs to the second; tracks personal records and weekly progression
- **Build optimization**: Tests different card combinations; maintains a mental model of synergies
- **Content creation**: Records clips of 8× combos, posts strategy guides, engages with community
- **Analytics tracking**: Exports run data (if available) to analyze win rates, order types, churn points

### Monetization Profile
- **Spending Tier**: Whale (~$15–30/month, with seasonal battle pass)
- **Purchase Drivers**: Cosmetic pass (if seasonal), exclusive cosmetics tied to leaderboard ranks, cosmetic card variants

### A Note About Casey
Casey is the longevity anchor and community voice. A small number of Caseys (2–5% of players) generate outsized engagement and content. Mitigation: Ensure leaderboards are stable and transparent; provide deep stats and replay data; add seasonal challenges or new card types every 4–6 weeks. Don't cater exclusively to Casey (will alienate Alex and Jordan), but don't ignore them either—they're your word-of-mouth.

## Persona Relationship & Design Tradeoffs

| Persona | Plays For | Risk If Ignored | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alex (Commute)** | Core feel (tight steering, instant reward, 5-min sessions) | 70% D1 churn | Core loop is **non-negotiable**. Every pixel of feel matters. |
| **Jordan (Collector)** | Progression feel, cosmetics, relaxation | Low retention post-week-1 | Keep cosmetics attainable; don't raise difficulty unexpectedly; make cards simple. |
| **Casey (Optimizer)** | Depth, leaderboards, build variety | High retention but zero referrals | Ensure mechanics are understandable; add seasons; keep balance updates transparent. |

A well-designed game serves Alex (primary), Jordan (retention), and Casey (community) *without compromise*. Example:
- **Core loop**: Alex's tight steering (5 sec to understand)
- **Cosmetics**: Jordan's attainable car skins every few runs
- **Leaderboards**: Casey's ranked tracking with transparent MMR
- **Cards**: Simple for Jordan ("Speed = faster"), deep for Casey (stacking synergies)
