# 01 — Target Audience (Delivery Rush)

## Overview

Delivery Rush targets casual mobile players seeking quick, satisfying arcade sessions with minimal onboarding friction. The game is designed for touch-first play (portrait 9:16) but also supports keyboard. A typical player session lasts 45–90 seconds, fitting perfectly into commute, lunch, or waiting-room moments.

## Psychographic Deep Dive

### Play Context
Delivery Rush players are **time-starved but engaged.** They play during:
- Morning commute (10–15 min)
- Lunch break (5–10 min)
- Before bed or between tasks (5 min)
- Occasional weekend wind-down (10–30 min)

No player expects to dedicate an evening to Delivery Rush. Sessions are *interruptible*—a player must be able to quit mid-run or restart instantly with zero friction. Platform must not require login, account creation, or tutorials on second launch.

### Play Motivation
**Primary**: Score attack and "one more run" loop. Casual arcade players chase combo multipliers, best scores, and personal-record runs. The thrill is *almost* getting a 8× combo before crashing. No narrative or story investment; pure score dopamine.

**Secondary**: Collection (vehicle cosmetics, card unlocks). Players upgrade to faster cars and equip stat-boosting cards not for competitive advantage, but for visual novelty and progression feel.

**Tertiary**: Leaderboards. A small percentage (<10%) will replay runs to top the weekly/global leaderboards; most casuals view leaderboards as optional flavor.

### Tolerance for Friction
Delivery Rush players have **zero tolerance for onboarding bloat, account systems, or gatekeeping.** 
- No tutorials longer than 2 minutes
- No login screens
- No mandatory cosmetics, ads, or paywalls before first play
- Core game fully playable free forever
- First delivery within 15 seconds of app load

### Spending Profile
**Whales**: ~5% of players; spend $10–30 over 3 months on cosmetics (rare skins, battle pass equivalent)  
**Dolphins**: ~25% of players; spend $2–10 per month on occasional cosmetics + rewarded ads  
**Free Players**: ~70% of players; play entirely free; watch rewarded ads for 2× coins (optional)

No pay-to-win mechanics. Speed, handling, and gameplay balance are identical regardless of cosmetics owned.

## Experience-Level Segmentation

**Casual (85% of audience)**:
- 20–60 min/week
- 45–90 sec per session
- Drop-in, no progression requirement
- Enjoys mastery without grind
- Uses mobile during brief windows

**Midcore (14% of audience)**:
- 90 min–4 hrs/week
- May chase global leaderboard top-10
- Interested in card synergies and build optimization
- Plays 5–10 runs per session

**Hardcore (1% of audience)**:
- 5+ hrs/week
- Speedrun potential, frame-perfect steering
- Community-focused; streams or creates content

Design priority: **Casual first.** Deliver a 45-second perfect-feel arcade game. Midcore/hardcore depth emerges from combo mechanics and card builds without gatekeeping casuals.

## Competitive Landscape Audience

**Comparable titles and their audiences**:
- **Crossy Road**: 18–45, all genders, mobile-first, 10–30 min/week sessions, cosmetic-driven
- **Crazy Taxi**: 20–50, male-leaning, arcade nostalgia, score-attack driven
- **Flappy Bird clones**: 15–40, all genders, ultra-casual, 2–5 min sessions

Delivery Rush sits between Crossy Road's cozy charm and Crazy Taxi's frenetic pace, targeting players who want *mastery without pressure*.
